- New mobile-first screen, a "practice companion" for use right before and during a dance practice session. Sits alongside the existing Kanban board (kept as-is, desktop-oriented) — a header toggle switches between the two for a given board. See [[Tables]] for the column/card model this builds on — the columns' `due_offset_days` / `is_due_column` / `is_quick_target` rules *are* the scheduling model here.
- ## Why
	- The board is built for organizing/documenting at a desk. Mid-practice, on a phone, the need is different: quickly build a practice queue, reorder on the fly, glance at notes/video, log quick thoughts, mark things done — without navigating away, because real practice is messy (fatigue, interleaving, re-planning mid-session).
- ## The session model — **continuous, no day boundary** (decided 2026-07-21)
	- This surface is a **continuous work model**. There is no concept of today or tomorrow, no session expiry, and `practiceSessionStore.ts` deliberately contains no `Date`. The key is `practice-session:{boardId}` and it persists until the cards leave it.
	- Two states, and they are the whole model:
		- **being trained** — queued to practise now
		- **trained, awaiting sort** — practised, and needing to be sorted back into spaced repetition via Session Review
	- Vocabulary follows: the container is the **practice queue**, and the done group carries a **"TO SORT (n)"** header — in dim ink, not `--pc-good`. Green means *scheduled* everywhere else in this palette and these are precisely the cards that aren't scheduled yet, so the colour was contradicting the label. The list header counts the two groups separately (**"2 to go · 1 to sort"**) rather than summing them; one number read as "N left to drill" when half of them were already practised. The word *"today's"* is wrong on this surface and comes out of all seven strings that used it.
	- Rejected: stamping the session and expiring it at local midnight. `resolveCard()` already empties a session you run through Review, so expiry could only ever delete an *unfinished* one — and the done-but-unreviewed cards it would discard are exactly those whose practice hasn't reached the schedule yet. See [[UX Batches 2026-07]].
	- The **cards** still have real dates (`due_date` is the actual spaced-repetition schedule). It's the *session* that has no day concept, not the app. That's why the empty state can honestly report due and overdue counts.
- ## Design direction
	- Color strategy: **Committed** — one saturated color carries real surface weight, related to but distinct from the board's ember/status family.
	- Dark/near-dark base (unlike the board's warm-light theme) — this view is short-glance, variable-light use, not long reading, so the earlier "dark is fatiguing" verdict (which was about the board) doesn't apply here.
	- No named anchor references; direction is originated for this view.
- ## Structure — four surfaces
	- ### Companion view (primary, persistent)
		- Pinned focus card (current skill: notes, video link, quick-note field, Done action) + a vertical, reorderable session list below it (~1/3 screen by default, expandable to full height on demand). Full-bleed content, no card chrome.
		- The focus card's identity block (status, title, history) is **sticky** to the top of the scroller. A long note otherwise pushed the title out of view entirely, so scrolling to the bottom of a note left you reading an anonymous document. `PracticeFocusCard` takes a `head` prop for this — `inline` (default), `sticky` (this view), `none` (Session Review, which draws the identity in its own pinned header).
		- **Every surface here names a card's parents alongside its name** — see [[Card Relationships]] for the two registers and the shared `ancestorNamesByCardId` map. The focus card and Session Review's head take it as the title's first line; the queue rows, add drawer and quick add take a 10px mono sub-line. Without it the leaf-only name makes two different drills the same string mid-session, which is how you practise the wrong one or file it at the wrong interval.
			- The counter-translate below moved from `.row-title` to a `.row-text` wrapper when the sub-line arrived: both lines are the card's name and have to hold still together.
		- **Sizes are in `dvh`, never `vh`.** `--session-list-height` was `34vh` inside a `100dvh` board, and `vh` is the *large* viewport — measured with the browser chrome retracted. With the chrome showing, the list claimed 34% of a taller viewport than it lived in and pushed its own last row below the fold; the same mismatch on `body { min-height }` scrolled the whole page and carried the topbar off the top. Both fixed 2026-07-26. Only confirmable on a real phone: on desktop the two units are identical.
		- Tap a row → becomes the focus card. Long-press/drag a row → reorder. Swipe a row left → a **Remove** button is uncovered behind it; tapping it drops the card from the queue.
			- **Swipe right to put it back.** An open row takes the gesture in either direction and snaps shut past the same halfway threshold; tap-anywhere-to-close is retained. Without it the only way to change your mind was a tap that nobody guesses is available.
			- The row's title is **counter-translated** while the row slides, so the card's name holds still and stays readable. Translating the whole row pushed the name off the left edge, which meant confirming a Remove against a blank strip. `.row-slot` also clips (`overflow: hidden`) — without it a swiped row slid past the list padding and off the viewport.
			- The action stays `--pc-overdue` red. Reversible-but-loud versus irreversible-but-quiet is a real inversion against Session Review, but the red was kept deliberately: the reveal-then-tap gesture is already the confirmation step, and dimming it invites the mis-swipe the gesture exists to prevent.
			- Remove takes the card out of the *session* — `session.removeCard`. It does **not** delete the card, its notes or its schedule; the board is untouched and re-adding it costs one search. A real delete was considered and rejected for this surface: mid-practice a mis-swipe is one thumb-width away, and the queue is the one place in the app where nothing is permanent.
			- Pending rows only. The "TO SORT" group deliberately has no swipe — pulling a practised card out there would skip Session Review, which is the one step that writes the schedule.
			- One gesture, three outcomes, decided by the first 10px: horizontal wins → swipe, vertical/held → the existing long-press drag, released early → focus. A tap anywhere while a row is open just closes it.
			- Two CSS constraints, both learned the hard way: the row needs `touch-action: pan-y` (the list scrolls vertically, so the browser has to be told to hand horizontal movement to the pointer handlers), and the "follow the finger" rule sets `transition-duration: 0s` rather than `transition: none` — see the `.dragging` note below for why removing `transform` from a rule that outranks `.row-move` silently kills the FLIP reorder.
		- The `⠿` grip is a **real drag handle**: pressing it lifts the row immediately, with no 350ms wait. Long-press-anywhere is retained alongside it. It previously signalled something false — drawn in `--pc-border` at 1.51:1, and not a handle at all, since only the long press did anything.
		- The other rows animate out of the way via FLIP. `reorder` fires live during the drag, so without it the rows teleport into their new positions and the reorder can't be followed. Same technique as the add drawer's browse list.
		- Mode is remembered per board (`practice-view-mode:{boardId}`), matching the `practice-session:{boardId}` convention — a reload mid-practice used to drop you onto the Kanban board.
		- Tap "Done" on the focus card → demotes it below a divider in the list (dimmed, still reachable/reopenable), no rating prompt, auto-advances focus to the next pending card.
		- Done tap gives a brief confirm receipt (haptic + a green flash on the FAB, reusing the drag-start vibrate precedent) and the focus card clears with a quick fade/slide rather than an instant cut — the one motion moment in this view, since it's the highest-frequency action per session.
		- **Correction (2026-07-26):** the notes field used to be a bespoke full-height textarea with its own click-to-caret and blur-save logic, auto-saving on collapse. That's gone. `PracticeFocusCard` now renders the card's `description` through the same shared `MarkdownNote` component the card modal uses — per-block inline editing, not a whole-field swap. See [[Card Notes]] for the editing model (pointerdown/pointerup gesture, blur-commits-immediately-closes-later, the coarse-pointer formatting bar) and for the component's theming API. `onBeforeUnmount` calls the component's `flush()` to commit an open edit before a card swap (this view's own focus-swap transition, or leaving Session Review) tears the instance down.
		- Session state (the queue, its order, done flags) is ephemeral — ***not*** synced server-side or kept as history — but persisted to `localStorage` per board so a reload/PWA background-kill mid-practice doesn't lose it.
	- ### Quick add (`PracticeQuickAdd.vue`) — the header search, one skill at a time
		- The magnifier in the board header is now in *both* modes, same affordance, different destination: on the Kanban board a hit opens the [[Card Notes]] modal, in practice it drops the card straight into the queue. Sharing a control across the two palettes means re-theming it: `.topbar.practice-view` already restyles `.mode-btn`/`.bar-btn`, and the magnifier needed the same rule — inheriting `--color-ink` put it at roughly 1.1:1 on the dark bar, invisible until it was looked for. A panel, not a full-screen sheet — it opens near the top because the keyboard takes the bottom half of the screen the moment it does.
		- It is **not** a smaller add drawer, and doesn't replace it. The drawer is for *building* a queue — browse a column, see what's overdue, add five things. Quick add is for the single skill you already have the name of, most often one you thought of mid-session. That's why it has no tabs, no Added panel, and clears itself after every add.
		- Typing searches every card on the board by name (most-overdue-first, undated last — the same `byUrgency` order as the drawer). `↑`/`↓` move the highlight, `Enter` adds it, `Esc` clears the query and only then closes.
			- The highlight starts on the first **non-queued** result, not row 0. Queued cards stay in the list (see below), so starting at 0 could land Enter on a row whose handler returns immediately — a keystroke that did nothing and said nothing.
			- **`Enter` never creates when something matched.** It used to fall through to the create row whenever the highlight didn't resolve, including the case where every match was already queued — so typing a prefix of a card that was already in the session and pressing Enter filed a second, half-named card. Creating from a query that matched something is now a deliberate tap; Enter only creates when there were no results at all. Caught in browser testing, which filed a card called `dro`.
			- Anything that isn't a successful queueing gets a dim notice line in the same slot as the `✓` receipt: *"Every match is already queued."*, or a failure message when the insert returns null (offline, RLS) — that path used to return silently, so a failed create looked identical to a tap that never registered. After an add the query resets and focus stays, so the next name can be typed immediately; a `✓ {name} queued` line is the receipt, since the row it came from is gone.
		- Cards already in the queue stay in the results, dimmed with a `✓`. Hiding them (what the drawer does, where the Added panel shows them instead) would make the skill you just typed look like a no-match.
		- **Create.** With at least three characters typed, no exact-name match, and an inbox column set, the last row is `+ Create "{query}" in {inbox}` — it creates the card there and queues it in one tap. The three-character floor exists because at one character every prefix that wasn't an exact match looked creatable. Practice is when new skills get named, and the board isn't reachable mid-session.
			- It waits for the real inserted row rather than using `addCard`'s optimistic temp id: that id is negative, and the session persists ids to `localStorage`, so a reload would restore a queue pointing at nothing.
			- With **no inbox column set** the row is replaced by a hint pointing at column settings. Deliberately no fallback column — see `is_inbox_column` in [[Tables]].
			- A created card needs no special handling downstream: it's in the queue like any other, so practising it marks it done and Session Review sorts it out of the inbox into a real column, which is exactly the triage the original inbox proposal asked for.
	- ### Add/Remove drawer (full-screen sheet over the companion view)
		- A pinned "Added" section stays on top regardless of which column tab is active. Tabs ordered Due column first, then quick-target columns, then the rest.
		- The Added panel is **always rendered at a fixed height** (`clamp(96px, 14vh, 270px)` — two rows' worth at the floor, still five at the ceiling), empty or not. The floor was 200px, which held a quarter of a 390px screen permanently for one italic "nothing yet" line while the browse list you came here to use started below the midpoint. Two reserved slots stop the layout jump; five hold the screen hostage. It used to be `v-if`'d on the first add with a `max-height`, which meant every add grew it and shoved the tabs and browse list down 51px. Reserving the space unconditionally is the point; a `max-height` alone just moves the jump to the first tap. See [[UX Batches 2026-07]].
		- The browse list is a `TransitionGroup`. Removing the tapped row still closes the list by a full 48px — that's unavoidable while rows leave the list — but the row fades out of flow and the rows below slide up via FLIP over 180ms, so the movement is trackable instead of instant. The alternative (leaving rows in place, checked) was rejected because it shows each added card twice.
		- Checkbox-style add/remove into the practice queue. No reordering here — reordering only happens in the companion view itself.
		- This drawer, not the empty state, is where a queue actually gets built. Auto-seeding ("start with the N most overdue") was proposed by the critique and **rejected** — skills are chosen by what you're about to physically drill, not by overdueness. That decision is what makes the drawer the priority surface. See [[UX Batches 2026-07]].
		- Every row carries `dueLabel()` text (`56d late`, `today`, `in 4d`). Without it the rows are undifferentiated: in the Due tab every status dot is red, so the dot carries no information and the row is just a name.
		- Tabs are sorted most-overdue-first, undated cards last (keeping board order among themselves). The **Added panel is never sorted** — it holds the order you added. Note the drawer was not urgency-ordered before: `cardsByColumn` sorts by `position`, and `sweepDueCards` only lifts *newly* swept cards to the top, so the order was "most recently swept, then however you'd dragged things".
		- Search spans **all** cards on the board, not the active tab; tabs hide while a query is active. Keyboard: the highlight starts on the first result, `↑`/`↓` move it, `Enter` adds the highlighted card and **clears the query while keeping focus** — so you type the next skill name straight away — and `Esc` closes. The board's own search (`BoardView.vue:60-95`) gets the same highlight and arrows; its `Enter` used to fire on a hardcoded `searchResults[0]`.
	- ### Session Review (manual, sequential triage)
		- A "Review session" button appears once ≥1 card is marked done. Never auto-prompted.
		- **Three fixed zones, and the panel itself never scrolls.** A pinned header (progress, card title, status, history line, ✕), a scrolling note in the middle, and a pinned bucket tray at the bottom. Only the note moves. This is the app's one irreversible write with no undo, so the thing being decided and the controls that decide it both have to be on screen at the moment of the tap — the earlier single-scroll layout pushed the title, the history line *and* the ✕ off the top while you scrolled down to reach the buttons, leaving you choosing an interval for a card you could no longer see. `Escape` closes the panel alongside `useBackButtonClose`.
			- The note stays **full-length and editable**, not collapsed behind a toggle — notes get written during review, not just read. Collapsing it was considered and rejected for that reason.
			- The tray hides while a note block is being edited. `MarkdownNote`'s formatting bar is also `position: fixed`, pinned above the on-screen keyboard, and two stacked bars over a keyboard leave almost nothing of the note visible. You are not choosing an interval and typing at the same time. The signal is an additive `update:editing` emit on the shared component (see [[Card Notes]]).
		- One card at a time: quick-target columns (`is_quick_target`, see [[Tables]]) as one-tap buckets — reuses the exact mechanic the board's drag-and-drop quick-move bar already uses (`store.moveCard`), just triggered by tap instead of drag. Plus an explicit **"Keep in {column}"** (labelled with the card's actual column), and a **"choose another column"** fallback for non-quick-target destinations.
			- **The buckets render as a ladder, not a set of names.** Each scheduling bucket shows its `due_offset_days` under the column name in mono — `Wöchentlich` / `7d` — and the non-scheduling quick targets are split below a hairline under an **"Off schedule"** label. Order needs no work: `quickTargetColumns` already sorts by `position`, and the ladder columns are in interval order on the board. Without the numbers the grid is a set of proper nouns whose sequence you have to already know, which is invisible to everyone except the person who configured the columns; with a parking column sitting after the longest rung it actively reads as "even longer than 60d" when it means "off the schedule entirely". Nothing is hardcoded — a board that grows a new rung shows the right number with no code change.
			- **"Keep" is not a no-op on the schedule.** It used to skip `moveCard` entirely, leaving the due date untouched — so a card you practised and kept went on accumulating lateness forever, and its "14d late" measured how long ago you first fell behind rather than how long since you practised it.
				- It now calls `store.recordPracticeInPlace()`, which applies the card's **own column's** `due_offset_days` and writes the two scheduling-history fields (see [[Tables]]). For the Today column, whose offset is `null`, that is exactly "stamp today" as agreed. For a card kept in a ladder column it means "due again in that column's interval" — stamping today there would make the column's interval a lie and bounce the card out on the next sweep. No column config was changed. See [[UX Batches 2026-07]].
				- **An undated card in a column with no rule stays undated.** Those are the parking columns — Library und Backlog, Ziele — where a card is deliberately outside spaced repetition. The first implementation stamped today there too, which would have enrolled a backlog note without being asked and swept it into the due column the next day; caught in browser testing. `last_practiced_on` is still written: the practice happened regardless of what the schedule says.
			- The completion screen counts real moves — *"3 rescheduled · 2 kept in place"* — not the queue length, which counted every keep as a sort.
			- Each card carries its scheduling history above the buckets: *"Wöchentlich · practiced 8d ago"*. That line is the evidence for the choice being made — "it was weekly and it went well, move it up a rung". It lives on `PracticeFocusCard`, so the practice view gets it too; the card modal has its own copy.
			- Completion copy counts **actual moves**, not the queue length: *"3 rescheduled · 2 kept in Today"*. Counting the queue meant every Keep-in-Due inflated the total.
			- Review shows each card's previous rung — *"was in Wöchentlich · practised 8 days ago"* — from `cards.last_scheduled_column_id` / `last_practiced_on` (see [[Tables]]). That's what lets you reason "this was weekly and it went well, move it up". It has to be **stored**, not derived: every card in Review has been swept into Today, whose offset is `null`, so `due_date − due_offset_days` fails precisely where the information is wanted.
			- No undo and no destination toast — considered and declined. A mis-tap is silent and permanent; recovery is finding the card on the board and dragging it back.
		- Also allows editing the card's notes inline during review (confirmed in scope, not deferred).
		- Once the queue empties: a restrained completion state (checkmark badge + card count, no confetti) rather than a plain sentence — the one milestone in this flow, so it earns slightly more than the per-card motion budget.
